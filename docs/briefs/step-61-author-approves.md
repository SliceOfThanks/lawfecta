# Feature brief: the author approves

Step: 61   Spec references: step 2 (Library roles), step 2d (the document standard), the Lapt workflow handoff of 2026-11-19 (question 4 of Claude's evaluation)
Status: built in acceleration mode. Stacey 2026-11-20: "Author approves, but others can add comments for edits and admin/author approves."

## Who
Everyone in a firm's Library: the administrator (the firm owner and anyone named administrator), editors, commenters and viewers.

## What
The author of a document is the person who created it. The author may approve their own document and decide the changes other people suggest in comments, as long as they are still an editor of the Library. The administrator may do both, as before. Everyone else with the commenter role or higher leaves comments, with or without a suggested change.

## Where
- A document's page in the Library: the "Approve" button, the line "Author (approves)" in the details at the top, and the "Suggested edits" tab.
- The Library list: a document sent for review shows "Review and approve" to its author too.

## When
When a draft is ready, and each time someone suggests a change.

## Why
Until now only the Library administrator could approve. In a firm with staff, the person who wrote the process knows it best, and one administrator became the bottleneck.

## How it could go wrong
- Someone approves a document they did not write: refused. The right comes from who created the document, recorded when it was made, never from what the page sends.
- An author who was moved down to commenter or removed from the team keeps approving: refused. The author must still be an editor.
- An author removes an attorney decision point from their own document: still only the administrator may accept that change.
- Approval skips the checks: the same blocks apply to the author (blocking quality items, images due).
- Another firm learns who wrote a document: it learns nothing.

## Settings and switches it uses
None new. Migration 0143.

## Questions, and how Claude answered them (acceleration mode)
1. Who is the author? **The person who created the document.** It is recorded already and cannot be changed from the page. Handing authorship to someone else is not built.
2. Must the author hold a role? **Yes, editor or higher.** A commenter cannot edit, so they cannot approve.
3. May an author accept a change that removes an attorney decision? **No, the administrator only**, as before. That rule protects the attorney's sign-off.
4. Who is told when a document is sent for review or a change is suggested? **The administrators and the author.**
5. Firm guidance for DELA (step 46): **stays with the administrator.** It applies to every document, so it has no single author.
6. Archive, trash and the team list: **stay with the administrator.**

## Success looks like
- An editor creates a document, sees "Author (approves): their name (you)" and can approve it.
- Another editor opens that document, has no "Approve" button, and can comment with a suggested change.
- The author accepts or rejects that change on "Suggested edits".
