DELA ATTORNEY UX RETEST AND CHANGE REVIEW
November 6, 2026
Baseline: DELA-attorney-ux-review.txt from this chat.
Site: https://hirelegalhelp.com/dela

OVERALL ASSESSMENT

There are substantial visible improvements. Costs, approved documents, file identification, talent access and several navigation paths are clearer. The Library is structurally better, but it still needs a visual and interaction redesign. The user's latest feedback is that the revised Library remains unsatisfactory. Do not close that item simply because the table columns now align.

The next development pass should prioritize consistency of the information and destinations, then the Library workspace design. In particular, monthly milestone dates disagree, action queues disagree, and Library suggestion counts disagree with the document view. Booking now has grouped times, but still renders too many dates and choices at once.

This is a change review of the current interface, not proof that a particular deployment caused each difference. No build identifier was available. The sample data also changed between reviews. For example, the monthly engagement is now paid and active, and standing arrangements have been added. Those data changes are not attributed to actions taken during this retest.

WHAT WAS TESTED

Read-only inspection covered Home, Get set up, Ask DELA, Library, approved documents, Files, Projects, agency proposals, Matches, gig files, one-off agreements, monthly work, Talent, a freelancer profile, Inbox, booking, Meetings, Notices, Profile, AI settings and Connect.

- Entering an unsaved $300 to $500 gig budget showed an estimated attorney total of $315 to $525.

- Opening the Milestones information control with Enter worked. Escape closed it.

- Opening Book a meeting displayed available slots. One Show more times control was exercised. No time was selected or booked.

Desktop and phone layouts were inspected. A 1440 by 1000 desktop viewport and a 390 by 844 phone viewport were requested. The browser's actual narrow widths varied during the session, so phone findings are reported as narrow-layout observations rather than a breakpoint certification. Measured Library page width did not exceed its viewport. A narrow monthly view also showed no measured horizontal overflow.

No save, send, signature, payment, hiring, cancellation, invitation, AI generation, OTP email or connection action was submitted. The confidential-materials code gate was read but not opened. The browser's temporary viewport override was reset and the Library was left open. The account remains signed in for the user to inspect it.

LIMITS

RECOMMENDED NEXT WORK, IN PRIORITY ORDER

R01. Make monthly dates and scope agree everywhere
Impact: High. Effort: Medium. Type: Consistency.
Where: Monthly engagement, The agreement > Milestones, operational Milestones cards and Included each month.
Observed: Week 1 is due Nov 13 in the agreement table and Nov 12 in the work card. Week 2 says Nov 20 versus Nov 19. Week 4 says Dec 4 versus Dec 7. The displayed paid month ends Dec 5 and next billing is Dec 6. The scope card excludes court filings, while The agreement says exclusions are not specified.
Why it matters: The attorney and provider can read different dates and boundaries on the same page. A due date after renewal needs explanation, not an implicit assumption.
Recommendation: Render agreed dates and exclusions from the same signed-version records. If an operational date differs, show the reason and governing agreement version beside it. Do not silently substitute a recalculated date. Use one milestone list for agreed outputs and working status, rather than duplicating two competing schedules. For existing sample records, repair the fixture or explain the difference before marking the issue resolved. This review cannot determine whether the root cause is sample data or application logic.
Acceptance check: Every repeated milestone has the same ID, version and due date. Any permitted exception is explicitly labeled. Exclusions match the signed scope. A month-end boundary test covers a final unit falling after renewal.
Attorney benefit: A dependable schedule and clearer review obligations.
Freelancer and agency benefit: Fewer deadline and scope disagreements.
Expected use change: Attorneys can rely on the operational page instead of rechecking terms.
Source: Designer judgment. Consistent representation is supported by NN/g heuristic 4: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ .

R02. Reconcile Notices with the actual work queue

Observed: Notices said "Needs your action (0)" and "Nothing waits on you right now." Projects simultaneously showed pending signature tasks, drafts and a gig with two proposals to review.
Why it matters: The new action queue can falsely reassure an attorney that nothing needs attention. Not every draft must be urgent, but pending signatures and unread proposals need consistent treatment.
Recommendation: Use a shared definition of actionable work. Separate required decisions from optional drafts, and show that distinction in both places. Derive the zero state from current work, not only the set of notice types categorized as actionable. If Notices intentionally covers a narrower subset, say "No unread action notices" and link to the full Projects queue instead of claiming nothing waits.
Acceptance check: The same pending signature appears in both queues, disappears after completion, and does not reappear from older history. An open proposal-review task is discoverable from Notices or a visible link to Projects.
Attorney benefit: Less chance of overlooking a decision.
Freelancer and agency benefit: Faster proposal and agreement responses.
Expected use change: The notification badge becomes a reliable return point.
Source: Designer judgment. Status feedback principle: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ .

R06. State consistent client-information boundaries beside AI inputs
Impact: High. Effort: Small. Type: Trust and safety.
Where: Ask DELA, Talent task description, firm-description helper, Profile and Confidential materials.
Observed: Some global banners prohibit client names, case numbers and privileged details. The newer warning names sensitive identifiers and says "Share a link from your own storage instead." Talent's new AI task box has no nearby privacy reminder. Confidential materials explicitly permits client names and case facts while stating AI never reads that area.
Why it matters: An attorney can mistake secure human document sharing for permission to paste a sensitive link into an AI box.
Recommendation: Use one clear boundary for operations AI inputs: "Use placeholders for client names and case details. Never enter sensitive identifiers, account details, health information or passwords." Put it beside every AI writing box. Remove the generic secure-link instruction from AI inputs. Product and security owners must verify the claimed isolation; this review did not test it.
Acceptance check: Every new AI composer includes the local reminder. No AI reminder implies that sending a sensitive storage link to AI is a safe substitute. The product's actual data handling agrees with its labels.
Attorney benefit: Less ambiguity about what can be entered where.
Freelancer and agency benefit: Clearer, safer handoff instructions.
Expected use change: Attorneys can use operations AI with a more consistent mental model.

LIBRARY SECOND-PASS BUILD NOTES

The new mockup is deliberately a stronger departure from the current spreadsheet presentation. It illustrates a compact document workspace. It does not change the live site.

Desktop layout
1. Use a short page heading and subtitle. Avoid repeating the complete process-definition paragraph on every visit.
2. Put Search documents, New document and Import in one compact toolbar.
3. Use a sidebar for All documents, Needs attention, Approved and Drafts, then collections. Keep their counts accurate and do not treat draft and urgent as synonyms.
4. Show title-first rows. Use title, ID/type, status and review action as the default information. Avoid separate ID, owner and date columns in this default retrieval view.
5. Selecting a row reveals owner, version, review date and direct actions in a detail panel. A separate Open document action opens the full approved procedure.
6. Keep quality notes separate from suggested edits. Both need specific, tested destinations.
7. Put imports and recordings in a small secondary area; retain required review counts without letting administration dominate the collection.

Phone layout
1. Put search immediately below Library, then compact filters and New document.
2. Show title, ID/type, status and review action within each compact card.
3. Show owner and due date on one quiet line or inside Details. Do not repeat six field labels per document.
4. Keep tap targets generous. Do not force dates, titles or status into a shrunken desktop table.
5. Return from document review to the same search, filter and list position.

Visual treatment
Use Lawfecta's navy and maroon for identity and emphasis, blue for primary actions, quiet neutral surfaces for the collection, and meaningful status pills. Do not make every action equally prominent. Titles should carry the list. Keep the typography readable and let metadata recede without relying on low contrast.

Proposed wording
Page title: "Your firm's documents". The visual concept uses "Your firm's playbooks" as a tone direction; use the broader Documents label in production because the collection contains multiple types.
Subtitle: "Find the process. Check what needs attention. Put it to work."
Quality count: "2 quality notes" when that is the actual data being counted.
Suggested-edit count: "2 suggested edits" only for proposals awaiting acceptance.
Imported state: "Review imported draft".
Empty search: "No documents match your search." Action: "Clear search".
Missing review date: "Review not scheduled" with a relevant setup action.
Missing owner: "Owner not assigned".

AI help
Keep a small field-related AI improvement action in the selected-document detail panel. It proposes edits to an unsaved/new draft and never changes the approved version automatically. Keep the client-information reminder adjacent. Do not use AI to hide unresolved navigation or count inconsistencies.

What not to change
Keep the successful aligned metadata model, searchable IDs, explicit missing states, approved-version separation, keyboard row activation and visible review cues. The next pass should improve retrieval and reading rather than removing useful information.
